# The EatoBiotics Design Constitution

> Premium, warm, alive. The homepage is the benchmark; the body is the
> centrepiece; the palette is the brand.

Principles, not implementation. Pixel values live in the code; the reasons live
here.

---

## 1. Typography

- **Serif leads, sans supports.** Headlines are serif (warm, editorial,
  human — closer to a beautiful book than a SaaS product). Body, UI, and data
  are sans. A screen that leads with sans-serif marketing copy has drifted.
- **Hierarchy is generous.** One display-scale headline per section, an
  uppercase tracking-wide eyebrow above it, calm body copy below. Never three
  competing headline weights on one screen.
- **Sentences over fragments.** Copy teaches; it doesn't bark. "Your probiotic
  network brightened today" beats "Probiotics +4%".

## 2. Colour

- **The palette is law:** white and warm-white surfaces; lime, green, teal,
  yellow, orange accents. **No blue. No purple. Ever.** Blue/purple is the
  uniform of generic health-tech; its absence is part of the brand.
- Green speaks for growth and foundation, teal for depth and calm, yellow and
  orange for energy and warmth. Amber (never red-alarm) marks strain — the
  product never shames.
- Gradients travel the brand spectrum (lime → green → teal → yellow → orange)
  and are earned: CTAs, accent bars, living light. Never full-page washes of
  saturated colour.

## 3. Illustration

- **The body is the hero image.** White-background figure art dissolved into
  the page and brought alive with light — aura, beam, rising particles — is
  the signature. Photography of food is supporting cast; stock photography of
  people is banned.
- Illustrations explain biology gently: glowing pathways, drifting microbes,
  luminous digestive systems. Beautiful first, anatomically suggestive second,
  clinical never.

## 4. Space

- **Whitespace is a feature.** Sections breathe (generous vertical rhythm,
  wide gutters, one idea per band). Density is a failure mode; if a screen
  needs a table to fit, the screen is trying to say too much.
- Cards share one chrome: soft rounded corners, hairline borders, a brand
  gradient accent bar, shadows that suggest light rather than depth charts.

## 5. Dark and light

- **The product is light.** White/warm-white is the default world.
- **Dark is cinema.** Deep-green dark bands are reserved for the moments that
  deserve a stage: the living body stage, closing CTA bands, cinematic films.
  Dark used as a default theme would flatten the moments that need it.

## 6. Interaction

- Hover states reward curiosity: gentle lift, accent ring, label warming to
  the system's colour. Nothing jumps, nothing flashes.
- Everything is keyboard-reachable, ARIA-labelled, touch-friendly, and honest
  about state (open/closed, active, coming soon).
- "Coming soon" is styled with the same care as "live" — quiet confidence,
  never greyed-out apology.

## 7. Educational storytelling

- Pages are chapters, not dashboards: hero → idea → evidence → invitation.
- Numbers appear inside stories ("74 — and climbing since you added kefir"),
  never as bare stat tiles.
- Avoid the generic-SaaS toolkit: no feature grids of tiny icons, no corporate
  flowcharts, no dashboard screenshots as marketing.

## 8. The benchmark

- **The homepage sets the bar.** Any new surface should be indistinguishable
  in quality from the homepage — spacing, typography, gradients, imagery,
  motion. If a new page looks like it belongs to a different product, it does.
